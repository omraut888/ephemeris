## 1. Contributions, as falsifiable claims

### Which ones lead

| | Claim | Strength today | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | The forecaster is calibrated | Strong on synthetic data, untested on real data | **Leads, together with B** |
| B | It beats strong baselines on CRPS at the same or better coverage | Synthetic only, against baselines run on our own generator | **Leads**: the paper stands or falls on this |
| C | The forecasts are bit-identical across platforms | Proven for one golden feed on one Mac | **Secondary**: a short section and the artifact |
| D | Each component earns its place (ablations) | Partly measured (detector §6, floor §7) | Supporting evidence for A and B |

**Why A and B lead together.** The method on its own (A) is a composition
of known parts: a linear Kalman filter, Mehra-style covariance matching,
Page's CUSUM, a bootstrap of the tail. A reviewer will say so, and they
will be right. What is new is the problem-specific modeling:
- the paycheck enters as a control input and as a separate observation of
  `m`;
- the settled checkpoint, with the pending tail replayed on every sync;
- the lump bootstrap that hands back exactly what the Huber gate clipped;
- the CUSUM-triggered covariance jump, capped at `σ̂_s²`;
- the truncated start for the spend rate.

These matter only if they produce a forecast that is calibrated *and*
better than what a careful engineer would build without them. That is B.
Calibration on model-matched synthetic data is nearly a tautology (the
filter is the optimal estimator there), and the realistic generator was
written by the same author as the model. So **B on real data is the
result**, and without it the paper is a systems note with a toy
evaluation.

**Why C is secondary.** It is true and checked byte for byte, but the
techniques are known: pure-Rust `libm` instead of platform math, a
counter-based ChaCha8 stream per path, no `std_math`, a pinned lockfile.
The interesting part for readers is a consequence: a forecast computed on
a phone can be audited on a laptop, and every number in the paper can be
regenerated in a browser. That earns one section, a table, and the
artifact, not the title. Its evidence is also narrower than the claim
sounds (§1, C below).

### The claims, with the test that would falsify each

Thresholds are fixed here, before any real-data run, and this file is
committed first so its commit date records that.

**A1. Calibration under the model's own assumptions (synthetic,
model-matched).** Every central interval (50/80/90/95%) of the Monte Carlo
cone covers within ±5 points of nominal at 7, 30 and 90 days, regime
detection off; within ±8 points with the default tracker. *Already
asserted* in `crates/ephemeris-core/tests/forecast_calibration.rs`. The
paper presents it as a correctness check, not as evidence of usefulness.

**A2. Calibration on real data.** On the held-out Berka accounts (§2),
pooled coverage of every central interval is within ±5 points of nominal
at 7 and 30 days, and within ±10 points at 90 days. The 95% bootstrap
interval (resampling accounts) of each coverage must overlap that band.
Falsified if any cell's interval lies wholly outside it. Reported cell by
cell either way.

**B1. Sharpness at matched calibration.** On held-out Berka accounts, the
mean CRPS skill of the Monte Carlo forecast relative to each baseline
(§2) is positive at 30 and 90 days, with a 95% account-block bootstrap
interval excluding zero. At 7 days we predict no significant difference:
the balance is nearly known a week out. Falsified, per baseline and
horizon, by an interval that includes or lies below zero.
**The baseline that matters is the probabilistic schedule-aware one**
(B-SA+ in §2). If it ties or wins, the paper says so in the abstract.

**B2. The same on the realistic generator.** It is the same comparison
with the same baselines (100 seeds). It is weaker evidence because we
built the generator, and the paper labels it that way.

**D1. Components.** Each ablation in Table 5 (§3) is compared with the
full model on CRPS and on 90% coverage, on both datasets. The predicted
signs are:
- adaptive noise off (fixed priors): worse coverage on real data;
- lumps off: under-coverage at 80–95%, mostly at 30–90 days;
- spend floor off: no change except where the floor binds;
- regime detector off: CRPS about 8% *better* at 90 days when nothing
  changes, much worse coverage in the 4 weeks after a change.

A sign opposite to the prediction, with a CI excluding zero, falsifies
that component's claim, and the paper reports it.

**C1. Bit identity.** For the golden feed **plus 100 generated feeds and
option sets** (new; today it is one feed), the report bytes are identical
across every target the repo already builds:
- native macOS arm64, debug and release;
- `wasm32` under Node (V8);
- the shipped web bundle under Vitest;
- the Tauri IPC path;
- UniFFI from Swift on macOS;
- the iOS simulator.

Falsified by one differing byte. What it does **not** cover, stated in
the paper: x86_64 (unless the Docker run in §3 is approved), a physical
iPhone, JavaScriptCore on a device, and other compiler versions (the
toolchain is pinned in `rust-toolchain.toml`).

### Berka → Feed mapping

Each Berka account becomes its own feed with one account (`account: 0`);
the dataset has no linked accounts, so there are no transfers.

|---|---|---|

**Tagging, using only data on or before each origin.** It is rerun at each
origin, because a tag built on the full history leaks the future.
- **income**: the largest regular inflow, the same rule as the Plaid
  adapter. Pensions (`DUCHOD`) and monthly `PREVOD Z UCTU` credits will
  qualify. Cash deposits (`VKLAD`) usually won't.
- **scheduled**: recurring outflows: `SIPO` (household), `UVER` (loan),
  `POJISTNE` (insurance), `LEASING`, plus the statement fee `SLUZBY` and
  interest `UROK`/`SANKC. UROK`, each a stream.
- **discretionary**: everything else (cash withdrawals, card,
  one-off remittances, cash deposits).

The `order` table (standing orders) is a 1999 snapshot with no dates.
Using it to tag would leak orders created after the origin, so it is used
only to *score* our causal bill detection (precision and recall), which is
itself a small result.

### What Berka lacks, and what that means for the claims

- **No pending state and no authorized dates.** The checkpoint and
  provisional-tail machinery (design §2) is not exercised on real data.
  It stays validated on synthetic data only, and the paper says so.
- **Sparse activity.** About 235 transactions per account over a few
  years, so roughly 5 a month. Spending is lumpy cash withdrawals, not
  daily card purchases. The daily-Gaussian `c` model is misspecified here,
  and the lump bootstrap carries more weight. This is a hard test, not an
  easy one, and it is fair to report it as one.
- **Era and currency.** CZK, 1993–1998. The noise priors and clamps are in
  dollar-scale units (`σ_s²` clamp of 10⁶ means $1000/day). Proposal:
  rescale every amount by one fixed factor before ingest (1998 rate, about
  30 CZK/USD) and scale the results back. Coverage is invariant to this
  and CRPS scales linearly (decision 3 in §8).
- **Holidays.** Paydays shift by the US Federal Reserve calendar.
  Options are a Czech holiday calendar in the core or a weekends-only
  calendar (decision 2 in §8).
- **Pay schedules.** Mostly monthly, with no biweekly payroll. The
  weekly/biweekly paths are untested on real data.
- **Possible anonymization transforms** on amounts are not documented.
  This is noted as a threat to validity.

### Backtest protocol (pre-registered)

- **Split.** 20% of accounts, chosen by a hash of the account ID, form the
  *development* set. Every adapter or tagging decision is made on them.
  The other 80% are **held out**, and run once, after this plan and the
  adapter are frozen and committed. No tuning on the held-out set. If a
  bug forces a rerun, the paper says so.
- **Eligibility.** The account needs at least 180 days of history before
  its first origin, and at least 12 transactions in those 180 days. The
  funnel (accounts in → eligible → with detected income) is Table 3.
- **Origins.** Every 30 days, from day 180 of the account's history to
  90 days before its last date (or 1998-12-31).
- **Horizons:** 7, 30, 90 days. **Truth:** the balance after the last
  transaction on or before origin + h.
- **Metrics:**
  - coverage of the 50/80/90/95% central intervals;
  - CRPS, from a 200-quantile grid for Monte Carlo methods and in closed
    form for Gaussian ones;
  - mean 90% interval width;
  - RMSE and MAE of the median;
  - PIT histograms.
- **Aggregation.** CRPS is scale-dependent and accounts differ by orders
  of magnitude, so headline numbers are *skill scores against B-SA+*,
  averaged per account first, then across accounts. Raw CRPS goes in the
  appendix.
- **Uncertainty.** Forecasts from the same account are correlated, so all
  intervals use a bootstrap that resamples whole accounts (2000
  resamples, fixed seed).
- **Seeds.** The forecast seed for each origin is a fixed hash of
  (account ID, origin), so any single forecast can be reproduced alone.
- **Stratification.**
  - income detected / not detected (the "irregular income" limitation,
    measured);
  - activity tercile;
  - months of history at origin (the design §3 question: are short
    histories overconfident?);
  - origins within 4 weeks after a detector alarm.

**Baselines (every one, every cell):**

| ID | Baseline | Exists? |
|---|---|---|
| B-30 | 30-day average drift, Gaussian band | Yes (test code) |
| B-LIN | Least-squares line over 90 days | Yes |
| B-SA | Schedule-aware, deterministic: same detected schedule as ours | Yes |
| **B-SA+** | B-SA plus an empirical error distribution: the quantiles of its own h-day errors at this account's earlier origins (split-conformal style) | **New.** The strongest simple competitor; without it, B-SA's CRPS is just its absolute error, which is an unfair comparison |
| B-SEAS | Monthly-seasonal naive: the change over the same window one or three months earlier, with empirical quantiles | New |
| B-GLOBAL | A global quantile model (gradient-boosted quantile regression) trained on development accounts over lagged balance and flow features | **Optional** (decision 5): it needs Python and LightGBM |

**MoneyData case study.** The same protocol, with weekly origins over 7
years (about 350 origins). n = 1, so no claim about users in general.
There is one account, so the bootstrap resamples blocks of time, not
accounts. It shows the method on modern card-style spending in another
currency. Like Berka, it has no pending state.

**If neither dataset works out** (license refused, or Berka too sparse
for any method to be calibrated), the honest alternative is:
1. Lead with A1 and B2 on synthetic data, and *say in the title and
   abstract* that the evaluation is synthetic.
2. Release the generator and harness as a benchmark, with the realistic
   generator's departures from the model listed.
3. Present the paper as a method-and-systems paper with a stated plan for
   real-data validation, and do not claim usefulness.

That is a weaker paper, suitable for a workshop, not a journal.

## 4. Limitations, stated up front

These go in the introduction, as a short list, and again with numbers in
the discussion. Numbers are from design §6 and §7 today. The paper uses
regenerated ones.

1. **The first week after a regime change is overconfident.** After a
   doubling of spending, the 90% interval covers 44% (model-matched) and
   10% (realistic) of outcomes 7 days later. Detection needs the CUSUM
   delay plus the settlement lag.
2. **Spending drops are detected slowly.** Halving is a 0.75σ shift.
   - Model-matched: first alarm after a median of 31 days.
   - Realistic: usually no alarm at all, because skew hides it; recovery
     takes a median of 106 days, and 2% of runs recover within 30.
3. **The detector costs about 8% CRPS at 90 days when nothing changes**
   (realistic generator, 519 → 560), through 0.48 false alarms a year.
   It is on by default because a missed change is the worse failure.
   T5 measures whether that trade holds on real data.
4. **Irregular income is not handled.** Without a detected schedule, the
   forecast has no income at all. Gig and cash-deposit income is common in
   Berka; F7 shows the cost.
5. **One fetcher.** Only one device fetches from the bank (sync design
   §1.4), and iOS never fetches. It does not affect forecast quality, but
   a deployment with no desktop or CLI device has no fresh data. This
   belongs in the systems section.

Further limitations the paper must also state:
6. One primary income stream; no day-of-week effects in the model.
7. The bootstrap cannot produce a lump larger than the largest seen.
8. Depository accounts only; no credit cards.
9. The realistic generator and the model share an author (circularity).
10. The real data is 1990s Czech or a single UK account. Neither has
    pending transactions, so the pending-tail design is validated
    synthetically only.
11. Bit identity is shown on one Apple Silicon machine and a simulator,
    not on a phone or x86_64 (C1).
12. No user study: "calibrated" is a statistical claim, not a claim that
    people make better decisions.

## 6. Format and the single command

```
paper/
  PLAN.md               this file
  main.tex              ACM-compatible; one switch for single column or acmart
  sections/*.tex
  refs.bib
  results/              generated: CSV, plus tables/*.tex and numbers.tex
  figures/              generated: one PDF per figure
  figures/src/*.py      figure scripts (or *.tex for pgfplots, decision 1)
scripts/paper.sh        the single command
crates/ephemeris-eval/  new: sweeps, baselines, the Berka and MoneyData adapters, writers
```

- **No hand-typed numbers.** The evaluation binary writes
  `paper/results/numbers.tex`, holding one macro per number quoted in
  prose (`\newcommand{\detCrpsCost}{7.9\%}`), and `tables/*.tex` for
  every table. The text uses only those macros.
- **Every number carries its provenance.** Each results file starts with
  the git commit, the dataset SHA-256, the seed and the config.
- **`scripts/paper.sh`:**
  1. Check the datasets in `data/berka/` and `data/moneydata/`
     (gitignored) against pinned SHA-256s of a canonical sorted export.
     If they are missing, print the fetch instructions and stop. Data is
     never committed: Berka's terms are unknown, and MoneyData's CC BY
     needs only attribution, but we gain nothing by redistributing it.
  2. `cargo run --release -p ephemeris-eval -- all --out paper/results`
  3. Render the figures into `paper/figures/`.
  4. `latexmk -pdf paper/main.tex`. MacTeX 2026 with pgfplots is
     installed; Python 3.11 has matplotlib 3.10.1.

  Flags: `--quick`, `--no-ios`, `--pdf-only` (rebuild the PDF from
  committed results).
- **Reproducibility check:** `scripts/paper.sh` followed by
  `git diff --exit-code paper/results` must be empty. The results are
  committed so the diff *is* the check, and anyone can rebuild the PDF
  without the long run.
- **Where the code goes.** The sweeps move out of `ephemeris-core/tests`
  into `ephemeris-eval`. The asserted checks (±5 and ±8 points) stay as
  tests there, so `scripts/test-full.sh` still runs them. The core stays
  pure; the adapters are pure functions of file contents. Parsing
  Berka's semicolon-separated files by hand avoids a `csv` dependency;
  quoted fields there never contain the delimiter (step 0 checks this).
- **Git.** Small commits, the repo-local identity, no trailers, never
  push.

## 8. Decisions (approved 2026-11-13)

These replace the options this section listed. Where a decision differs
from what §2–§6 say, this section wins.

1. **Holidays: a pluggable calendar, chosen per feed.** The feed gets an
   optional `holidays` field (`"us"`, the default when absent, or
   `"cz"`). The core takes the calendar as a value; Czech rules are one
   implementation, not hard-coded. Golden outputs for existing (US) feeds
   must stay byte-identical, which the golden tests check.
2. **Currency: per-account normalization in the Berka adapter, no global
   rate.** The rule, fixed before any held-out run:
   - At each origin, take every *complete* calendar month of the
     account's history on or before the origin. The month's gross flow is
     `F = Σ |amount|` over its transactions.
   - The account's scale is `s = median(F)` over those months.
   - Every amount and balance in the feed is multiplied by `R / s`. `R`
     is a fixed reference: the median monthly gross flow of the realistic
     synthetic generator (seeds 0–99, months fully inside its year),
     computed once by a test and written into the code as a constant. So
     a normalized Berka account has the flow scale that the engine's
     priors and clamps were tuned on.
   - Forecasts are made in normalized units and every output (quantiles,
     truth, CRPS) is multiplied back by `s / R`. Coverage is unaffected
     by the back-scaling; CRPS is then in CZK per account.
   - The scale uses only data on or before the origin, so it leaks
     nothing from the future.
   - *Limitation, stated in the paper:* the engine's priors are absolute,
     so the result depends on this rule. It is a choice made once, not
     tuned.
3. **Baselines.** Add B-GLOBAL, a LightGBM quantile model in Python in
   `paper/` only, with pinned dependencies and never in the app. Also add
   **B-EQ**, an empirical-quantile baseline: the quantiles of the
   account's own past h-day balance changes, added to today's balance.
4. **Berka `authorized_date = date`** (the booking date).
5. **Ablations** run on a 25% sample of development accounts during
   development, and on all held-out accounts for the final run.
6. **x86_64 bit identity** runs in a GitHub Actions workflow on an x86
   runner, not in Docker on this Mac. The repo gets the workflow file; the
   user pushes it and checks the run.
7. **matplotlib** for figures, with versions pinned in
   `paper/requirements.txt`.
8. **Venue:** arXiv stat.AP (cross-list cs.LG, q-fin.GN), then ICAIF.

**Deviations found on development data (2026-11-13, for review).** Both
were fixed before any development result was read, and neither touched
held-out data.

1. **Berka's calendar is `every_day`, not Czech.** A first run over 16
   development accounts detected income at none of 541 origins. The dates
   explain it. Berka books on weekends at the uniform rate: 28.5–30.5% of
   records in every operation type, against 2/7 = 28.6%. A pension due on
   the 11th lands on the 11th even on a Sunday, which no business-day
   calendar can produce, so the regular-income test rejected every stream.
   The core therefore has a third calendar, `every_day` (no business-day
   shifts), and Berka uses it. Decision 1 stands as built (pluggable, per
   feed, US default, Czech available and tested). Only the choice for
   Berka changed. With `every_day`, income is detected at 357 of those 541
   origins.
2. **Accounts with inconsistent balances are excluded.** The CTU export
   stores amounts and balances as whole crowns, rounded separately, so
   the running sum drifts from the bank's balances by up to about 30
   crowns. That is harmless, and the running sum is used as the truth.
   But 19 of 890 development accounts (2%) differ by more than 50 crowns,
   and 18 of them by more than 1,000: a reported balance that resets
   mid-history. The rule: exclude an account whose running sum differs
   from every balance the bank reported that day by more than 100 crowns
   on any day (`berka::MAX_DRIFT_CZK`). It applies to both splits and is
   counted in Table 3.

**Data rules.**
- Raw Berka and MoneyData files are never committed. `data/berka/` and
  `data/moneydata/` are gitignored. A download script fetches them and
  checks their SHA-256s.
- The 20/80 development / held-out split is enforced in code by a
  deterministic hash of the account id.
- Held-out evaluation needs an explicit `--held-out` flag. Every such run
  is appended to the committed log `paper/results/heldout-runs.log`
  (time, git commit, arguments). A second run is refused unless it is
  given a `--rerun-reason`, which is logged too, so the log shows
  whether the held-out set was run once.
