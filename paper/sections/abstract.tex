\begin{abstract}
We forecast a person's bank balance \hA, \hB\ and \hC\ days ahead with
predictive distributions, using only that person's own transactions on
their own devices. The forecaster tracks balance, spending rate and
paycheck with a Kalman filter and simulates a Monte Carlo cone.

We evaluated it against a documented analysis plan and a timestamped model
freeze before the logged held-out evaluation. The plan fixed claims,
thresholds, baselines and a revision rule. That the plan preceded the first
development results rests on author-controlled commit times, while the
freeze's precedence over the held-out run is confirmed by GitHub's push
record. The original model failed its calibration claim on the development
accounts of the Berka bank data. The planned rule selected a revised
variant, V2, which models sparse discretionary spending as a compound
Poisson process. The main held-out evaluation then ran once, through a
guarded and logged command, on \R{ho/data/accounts_with_eligible_origin}
accounts.

On held-out accounts, V2 has lower CRPS than the probabilistic
schedule-aware baseline (mean per-account skill \R{ho/pair/B-SA+/30/skill}
at \hB\ days and \R{ho/pair/B-SA+/90/skill} at \hC\ days). It has higher
CRPS at \hB\ and \hC\ days than an empirical-quantile forecast of the
account's own past balance changes (\R{ho/pair/B-EQ/30/skill} and
\R{ho/pair/B-EQ/90/skill}) and than a gradient-boosted model trained across
accounts (\R{ho/pair/B-GLOBAL/30/skill} and \R{ho/pair/B-GLOBAL/90/skill}).
Pooling the scores over all origins gives the same signs. V2's \lC\%
intervals cover \R{ho/var/V2/7/cov90}--\R{ho/var/V2/30/cov90}\%, and it
misses below them less often than nominal. Its \lA\% intervals are too
wide (\R{ho/var/V2/7/cov50}--\R{ho/var/V2/90/cov50}\%), so the planned
calibration claim fails at that level. On a single UK account
(MoneyData), V2's \lC\% intervals cover only \R{md/main/V0/7/cov90},
\R{md/main/V0/30/cov90} and \R{md/main/V0/90/cov90}\%.
\end{abstract}
