\begin{abstract}
A person's bank balance can be forecast from their own transactions with a
state-space model, which tracks pay, bills and a spending rate, or with
nothing more than the empirical distribution of the account's own past
balance changes. We ask when each is better, on sparse booked accounts of
the Berka bank data.

\emph{The headline: a horizon reversal.} On held-out accounts, a Kalman-filter
Monte Carlo forecaster with compound-Poisson spending (V2) has lower CRPS
than that empirical forecast (B-EQ) at \hA\ days (skill
\R{ho/pair/B-EQ/7/skill}). B-EQ has lower CRPS at \hB\ and \hC\ days (V2's
skill \R{ho/pair/B-EQ/30/skill} and \R{ho/pair/B-EQ/90/skill}; pooled over
origins, \R{ho/pair/B-EQ/30/pooled_skill} and
\R{ho/pair/B-EQ/90/pooled_skill}). On accounts with under a year of history,
V2's \lC\% interval at \hC\ days covers
\R{ho/str/history-lt-12-months/V2/90/cov90}\% against B-EQ's
\R{ho/str/history-lt-12-months/B-EQ/90/cov90}\%.

\emph{The explanation (exploratory).} Every method that projects the detected
pay-and-bill schedule loses to B-EQ at \hB\ and \hC\ days. That holds even
when the schedule is combined with the same empirical residuals B-EQ uses.
With under a year of history the pooled gap is small or reversed, though
the account mean still favours B-EQ. In simulation, irregular schedules,
not sparse spending, give B-EQ its long-horizon advantage. Calendar alignment
and sparse event counts do not explain it.

\emph{Overdraft events (exploratory).} At \hB\ and \hC\ days no method
beats the account's own historical frequency of falling below zero
(prevalence \R{xh/s5/u0/90/prevalence}\% at \hC\ days), and V2 does not
at any horizon. Every method is too pessimistic about dips.

\emph{Calibration.} V2's \lC\% intervals meet their planned band. Its \lA\%
intervals cover too much, so the planned calibration claim fails.

The evaluation followed a documented analysis plan and a timestamped model
freeze before the logged held-out evaluation. That the plan preceded the
first development results rests on author-controlled commit times.
Analyses added after the held-out results are labelled exploratory.
\end{abstract}
