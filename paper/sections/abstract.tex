\begin{abstract}
A person's bank balance can be forecast from their own transactions with a
state-space model, which tracks pay, bills and a spending rate, or with
nothing more than the empirical distribution of the account's own past
balance changes. We ask when each is better, on sparse booked accounts of
the Berka bank data.

\emph{The result.} On sparse Berka accounts, a Kalman-filter Monte Carlo
forecaster with compound-Poisson spending (V2) has lower CRPS than that
empirical forecast (B-EQ) at \hA\ days, while the empirical forecast wins
at \hB\ and \hC\ days. On held-out accounts V2's account-mean skill against
B-EQ is \R{ho/pair/B-EQ/7/skill}, \R{ho/pair/B-EQ/30/skill} and
\R{ho/pair/B-EQ/90/skill} at \hA, \hB\ and \hC\ days (pooled over origins,
\R{ho/pair/B-EQ/7/pooled_skill}, \R{ho/pair/B-EQ/30/pooled_skill} and
\R{ho/pair/B-EQ/90/pooled_skill}). V2 also has higher CRPS than a
gradient-boosted model pooled across accounts (B-GLOBAL) at \hB\ and \hC\
days.

\emph{Tests of schedule replacement (exploratory).} Replacing the scheduled
flows in B-EQ's own past windows with the detected schedule's projection
(B-SR) raises CRPS at \hB\ and \hC\ days. Supplying realized future flows
from detected streams improved schedule replacement at \hB\ days. At \hC\
days, the oracle variant still scored worse than the empirical forecast
under both aggregations (account-mean skill
\R{cf/pred/p2/90/skill_mean@3}, pooled \R{cf/pred/p2/90/skill_pooled@3}, on
later Berka origins in a prespecified follow-up on previously evaluated
data). Shuffling historical flow--residual pairs incurred a much smaller
penalty than schedule replacement. These contrasts leave the source of the
remaining loss unresolved.

\emph{Shortfalls (exploratory, secondary).} For new negative
booked-balance crossings, the four unrecalibrated forecasts we tested all
lost to the account's own historical frequency at every horizon, and all
over-predict them.

\emph{Calibration.} V2's \lC\% intervals meet their planned band. Its \lA\%
intervals cover too much, so the planned calibration claim fails.

The evaluation followed a documented analysis plan and a timestamped model
freeze before the logged held-out evaluation. That the plan preceded the
first development results rests on author-controlled commit times.
Analyses added after the held-out results are labelled exploratory.
\end{abstract}
