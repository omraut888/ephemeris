\begin{abstract}
A person's bank balance can be forecast from their own transactions with a
state-space model, which tracks pay, bills and a spending rate, or with
nothing more than the empirical distribution of the account's own past
balance changes. We ask when each is better, on sparse booked accounts of
the Berka bank data.

\emph{The headline: a horizon reversal.} On held-out accounts, a Kalman-filter
Monte Carlo forecaster with compound-Poisson spending (V2) has lower CRPS
than that empirical forecast (B-EQ) at \hA\ days (skill
\R{ho/pair/B-EQ/7/skill}). B-EQ has lower CRPS at \hB\ and \hC\ days (V2's
skill \R{ho/pair/B-EQ/30/skill} and \R{ho/pair/B-EQ/90/skill}; pooled over
origins, \R{ho/pair/B-EQ/30/pooled_skill} and
\R{ho/pair/B-EQ/90/pooled_skill}).

\emph{What does not explain it (exploratory).} We did not measure
schedule irregularity in Berka, and these analyses followed the held-out
results. Replacing the scheduled flows in B-EQ's own past windows with the
detected schedule's projection (B-SR) raises CRPS at \hB\ and \hC\ days
(pooled skill \R{xh/s3/B-SR-vs-B-EQ/30/skill_pooled} and
\R{xh/s3/B-SR-vs-B-EQ/90/skill_pooled} on held-out accounts). But projection
error is not sufficient: on development accounts, replacing them with the
\emph{realized} future scheduled flows still loses to B-EQ at \hC\ days
(account mean \R{xd/s11/o-vs-f0/90/skill_mean}; pooled
\R{xd/s11/o-vs-f0/90/skill_pooled}). Breaking the pairing of each window's
scheduled and other flows costs B-EQ only a little, so within-window
dependence, our leading hypothesis, explains a small part at most; a
forward-period test on held-out accounts is specified but not yet run.
Effects of schedule predictability are weak and depend on how scores are
averaged.

\emph{Overdraft events (exploratory).} Most short-horizon negative-balance
events continue a balance that is already negative. For \emph{new}
crossings of zero (prevalence \R{xh/s5s/u0/90/new-crossings/V2/prevalence}\%
at \hC\ days on held-out accounts) no forecast beats the account's own
historical frequency at any horizon, and the four model-based forecasts
over-predict them badly (V2's mean probability at \hC\ days:
\R{xh/s5s/u0/90/new-crossings/V2/mean_p}\%).

\emph{Calibration.} V2's \lC\% intervals meet their planned band. Its \lA\%
intervals cover too much, so the planned calibration claim fails.

The evaluation followed a documented analysis plan and a timestamped model
freeze before the logged held-out evaluation. That the plan preceded the
first development results rests on author-controlled commit times.
Analyses added after the held-out results are labelled exploratory.
\end{abstract}
