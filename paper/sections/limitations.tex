\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Calibration at the centre.} V2's \lA\% interval covers
  \R{ho/var/V2/30/cov50}\% at \hB\ days. The selection rule checked only the
  \lC\% level. A user reading the cone's middle as a likely range is
  misled.
\item \textbf{Sharpness.} On Berka, an empirical baseline from the
  account's own history beats the model at \hB\ and \hC\ days.
\item \textbf{The first week after a change is overconfident}
  (\S\ref{sec:regime}), and downward changes in skewed spending are
  rarely detected.
\item \textbf{Irregular and one-off flows.} Without a detected income the
  forecast has no income term. Large one-off transfers, as in the MoneyData
  account, are outside the model.
\item \textbf{The data.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Berka is Czech, in crowns, from \R{berka/first_year} to
    \R{berka/last_year}, with sparse activity. Its anonymization may have
    transformed amounts in ways that are not documented, and its licence
    terms are not stated.
  \item MoneyData is one UK account.
  \item Neither has a pending state, so the settled-checkpoint design is
    validated on synthetic data only.
  \item Paychecks are mostly monthly, so the weekly and biweekly paths
    are untested on real data.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Normalization.} Each account is rescaled at each origin by
  its median monthly gross flow, so the engine's dollar-scale priors fit.
  Coverage and PIT are invariant to this, but the priors' effective
  strength varies with it, and a different reference scale could change
  results.
\item \textbf{Our generators.} The realistic generator and the model share
  an author, so its departures from the model are the ones we thought of.
  Method rankings on it did not carry over to Berka (the B-EQ reversal).
\item \textbf{The protocol.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The plan predates the real-data runs by its commit time only; the
    earliest independent evidence of it is the same push as the first
    development results.
  \item Held-out ablations and paired intervals were computed after the
    main held-out results were known.
  \item The ablations were of V0, not V2.
  \item B-GLOBAL's hyperparameters were fixed at common defaults, not
    tuned. A tuned model might win by more.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bit identity} is shown on one Apple Silicon machine for all
  targets. On x86\_64 it was shown for the golden feed before the fix
  (\S\ref{sec:identity}). It has not been shown on a physical phone or with
  other compiler versions. The evaluation's closed-form Gaussian CRPS
  still calls the standard library's \texttt{exp}. That affects scoring of
  the Gaussian baselines at the last digit across platforms, never a
  forecast.
\item \textbf{No user study.} ``Calibrated'' is a statistical statement,
  not a claim that people make better decisions with the cone.
\item \textbf{One primary income stream}, no day-of-week effects in the
  model, depository accounts only, and lumps no larger than the largest
  already seen.
\end{enumerate}
