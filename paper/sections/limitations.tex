\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Calibration at the centre.} V2's \lA\% interval covers
  \R{ho/var/V2/30/cov50}\% at \hB\ days. The selection rule checked only the
  \lC\% level. A user reading the cone's middle as a likely range is
  misled.
\item \textbf{The explanation is exploratory.} The analyses of
  \S\ref{sec:explanation} and \S\ref{sec:overdraft} were specified and run
  after the held-out results were known. They were developed on the
  development split, but they are not confirmatory. Schedule irregularity
  in Berka was not measured directly, and the simulation uses our own
  generator.
\item \textbf{Overdraft events.} Falling below zero is rare in Berka
  (\R{xh/s5/u0/30/prevalence}\% of held-out origins at \hB\ days), so its
  Brier scores rest on few events. The thresholds were fixed in our written
  specification, not chosen by users.
\item \textbf{Estimands.} The planned skill is a mean of per-account ratios,
  which accounts with small reference CRPS can move a lot. We report the
  pooled ratio beside it; signs agree, magnitudes do not.
\item \textbf{Scoring resolution.} V1, V4 and B-GLOBAL are scored from
  quantile functions that are flat beyond \R{c/qf-lo}\% and \R{c/qf-hi}\%,
  coarser in the tails than the Monte Carlo cones.
\item \textbf{The first week after a change is overconfident}
  (\S\ref{sec:regime}), and downward changes in skewed spending are
  rarely detected. On real data, the detector's effect could only be
  described, not tested.
\item \textbf{Irregular and one-off flows.} Without a detected income the
  forecast has no income term. Large one-off transfers, as in the MoneyData
  account, are outside the model.
\item \textbf{The data.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Berka is Czech, in crowns, from \R{berka/first_year} to
    \R{berka/last_year}, with sparse, booked activity. Its anonymization
    may have transformed amounts in ways that are not documented, and its
    licence terms are not stated.
  \item MoneyData is one UK account.
  \item Neither has a pending state, so the settled-checkpoint design is
    validated on synthetic data only.
  \item Paychecks are mostly monthly, so the weekly and biweekly paths are
    untested on real data.
  \item Our claims are therefore about sparse booked bank accounts, not
    modern card-heavy accounts.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Selection and look-ahead.} The drift exclusion uses each
  account's full history. B-GLOBAL and the V3/V4 priors are trained on
  data from all calendar dates.
\item \textbf{Normalization.} Each account is rescaled at each origin by
  its median monthly gross flow, against a constant taken from our
  realistic generator. Because the filter is refitted with fixed-unit
  priors, a different constant could change the forecasts.
\item \textbf{Our generators.} The realistic generator and the model share
  an author, so its departures from the model are the ones we thought of.
  Method rankings on it did not carry over to Berka (the B-EQ reversal).
\item \textbf{The protocol.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The plan predates the real-data runs by its commit time only.
  \item The ablations, the paired intervals, the pooled skills, the tail
    misses and the A2 verdicts per cell were computed after the main
    held-out results were known.
  \item The ablations were of V0, not V2.
  \item B-GLOBAL's hyperparameters were fixed at common defaults, not
    tuned.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bit identity} is shown for the tested matrix in
  \S\ref{sec:identity} only.
\item \textbf{No user study.} ``Calibrated'' is a statistical statement,
  not a claim that people make better decisions with the cone.
\item \textbf{The model's scope:} one primary income stream, no
  day-of-week effects, depository accounts only, and lumps no larger than
  the largest already seen.
\end{enumerate}
