\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Calibration at the centre.} V2's \lA\% interval covers
  \R{ho/var/V2/30/cov50}\% at \hB\ days. The selection rule checked only the
  \lC\% level. A user reading the cone's middle as a likely range is
  misled.
\item \textbf{The explanation is exploratory.} The analyses of
  \S\ref{sec:explanation} and \S\ref{sec:overdraft} were specified and run
  after the held-out results were known. They were developed on the
  development split, but they are not confirmatory. Schedule irregularity
  in Berka was not measured directly, and the simulation uses our own
  generator.
\item \textbf{Overdraft events.} Falling below zero is rare in Berka
  (\R{xh/s5/u0/30/prevalence}\% of held-out origins at \hB\ days), so its
  Brier scores rest on few events. The thresholds were fixed in our written
  specification, not chosen by users.
\item \textbf{Estimands.} The planned skill is a mean of per-account ratios,
  which accounts with small reference CRPS can move a lot. We report the
  pooled ratio beside it; signs agree, magnitudes do not.
\item \textbf{Scoring resolution.} V1, V4 and B-GLOBAL are scored from
  quantile functions that are flat beyond \R{c/qf-lo}\% and \R{c/qf-hi}\%,
  coarser in the tails than the Monte Carlo cones.
\item \textbf{The model's reach.} The first week after a change is
  overconfident, and downward changes in skewed spending are rarely
  detected (\S\ref{sec:regime}). Without a detected income there is no
  income term, and one-off transfers are outside the model. There is one
  primary income stream and no day-of-week effect.
\item \textbf{The data.} Berka is Czech, from \R{berka/first_year} to
  \R{berka/last_year}, with sparse booked activity, mostly monthly pay, no
  pending state, undocumented anonymization and unstated licence terms.
  MoneyData is one account. Our claims are about sparse booked accounts,
  not modern card-heavy ones.
\item \textbf{Selection, look-ahead and units.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The drift exclusion uses full histories, though a past-only rule
    selects the same origins on development (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).
  \item The original B-GLOBAL and the V3/V4 priors use data from all
    dates.
  \item The per-origin rescaling uses a constant from our generator, and
    a different constant could change forecasts.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Our generators} share an author with the model. B-EQ's
  ranking on them did not carry over to Berka.
\item \textbf{The protocol.} The plan predates the real-data runs by its
  commit time only. Every analysis after the main held-out run is
  exploratory. The planned ablations were of V0, not V2. B-GLOBAL was not
  tuned.
\item \textbf{Bit identity} is shown for the tested matrix only
  (Appendix~\ref{app:identity}).
\item \textbf{No user study.} ``Calibrated'' is a statistical statement,
  not a claim about decisions.
\end{enumerate}
