\section{The state-space forecaster}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{State and observations.} The state is
$x_t = [b_t, c_t, m_t]^\top$: the balance, the discretionary spending rate
per day, and the expected paycheck. On a daily grid,
\begin{equation}
x_{t+1} = A x_t + B u_t + w_t,\quad
A = \begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix},\;
B = \begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{bmatrix},\;
w_t \sim \mathcal N\!\left(0, \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_s^2, q_c, q_m)\right).
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Known flows.} Paychecks and scheduled bills enter $b$
  through $u_t$ at their actual amounts. A paycheck's amount is also an
  observation of $m$, with variance $R_{\text{inc}}$.
\item \textbf{The balance} is observed through $[1\;0\;0]$.
\item \textbf{Tagging.} An adapter tags each record from the data alone.
  The primary income stream is the largest regular inflow; a bill is a
  recurring outflow at a regular interval.
\item \textbf{Pending records.} A settled checkpoint with a replayed
  provisional tail handles them. None of our real data has them.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Adaptive noise, the gate and lumps.} Each innovation $\nu$ gives
a one-sample estimate of its channel's variance, for example
$\nu^2 - hP^-h^\top$ for the income channel. It is unbiased only under the
correct innovation model, before the gate, weighting and clamp; after them
it is a heuristic. The estimates are folded into an exponentially
forgetting weighted mean, $n \leftarrow \lambda n + w$,
$v \leftarrow \operatorname{clamp}(v + w(\hat v - v)/n)$, in the spirit of
covariance matching \citep{mehra1970,myers1976}. The weight $w = (v/S)^2$
discounts samples taken while the state is uncertain. A balance innovation
beyond a gate is treated as an unexplained \emph{lump} flow; the forecast
resamples the recorded lumps.

\paragraph{Regime changes.} A two-sided CUSUM \citep{page1954},
$g^\pm \leftarrow \max(0, g^\pm \pm e - \kappa)$ with an alarm when
$g > h$, runs on the normalized, gate-clipped innovations $e$. On an alarm,
the run's mean shift $\hat\Delta$ becomes a zero-mean jump of variance
$\hat\Delta^2$ in $c$ or $m$, and the noise estimate forgets back to its
prior count.

\paragraph{The Monte Carlo cone.} Each of \R{c/paths} paths starts from
$x_0 \sim \mathcal N(\hat x, P)$. It draws its noise variances from scaled
inverse-$\chi^2$ distributions whose degrees of freedom are the effective
counts $n$: a heuristic, not a posterior. Each simulated day applies
$b \mathrel{+}= -c + \varepsilon + \ell + \text{income} + \text{bills}$,
with $c$ and $m$ as random walks. Lumps $\ell$ are resampled at their
empirical rate. Paychecks land on the detected schedule with a sampled
offset. Bills recur on their day of the month or with a sampled drift. The
initial spend rate is truncated at zero, so that no path earns money every
day. Each path uses its own counter-based random stream, so a forecast is
reproducible from its seed (Appendix~\ref{app:identity}).

\paragraph{V2: compound-Poisson spending.} For an account whose median
number of discretionary records per complete month, over the
\R{c/sparse-window} days before the origin, is below \R{c/sparse-max},
discretionary flows are a compound Poisson process instead of
$-c + \varepsilon$ and the lumps:
\begin{itemize}
\item each path draws a rate
  $\lambda \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(n + \tfrac12, \text{rate} = D)$, the Jeffreys
  posterior for $n$ events in $D$ days;
\item each day then has $N \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)$ events, whose
  signed amounts are resampled from the window.
\end{itemize}
On Berka the rule fired at every held-out origin
(\R{ho/vdata/origins_sparse_v2} of \R{ho/vdata/origins}). The option is
off in the engine's default configuration. The original model, V0, is the
same forecaster without it. The other revision candidates are described in
Appendix~\ref{sec:variants}.
